The honest answer to "how much should I spend on Facebook ads?" is: less than you think to start, and more only once the numbers say so.

Most small businesses that lose money on Meta ads (Facebook and Instagram) don't lose it because the budget was too small. They lose it because they spent a big budget before they knew which ad, audience and offer actually worked.

## A sensible starting budget

For a local business such as a café, salon, trade or clinic, I'd start most clients at **$15 to $30 a day**. That's roughly $450 to $900 a month, paid straight to Meta.

That's enough for Meta's system to get past its "learning phase" (usually the first one to two weeks) and give you real numbers. It's small enough that if something isn't working, you've learned that cheaply.

Spending $5 a day usually isn't enough to learn anything. Spending $100 a day on day one is how people end up with an expensive lesson.

## What clicks and leads tend to cost in Australia

These are published benchmarks. Treat them as a rough guide, because your real numbers depend on your industry, your offer and how good the ad is.

| Measure | Typical range in Australia | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| Cost per click (CPC) | about $1–$3 | What you pay each time someone clicks the ad |
| Cost per 1,000 views (CPM) | about $8–$25 | What it costs for your ad to be shown 1,000 times |
| Cost per lead | about $15–$65 | What it costs to get an enquiry, booking or sign-up |

Finance, health and other competitive industries sit at the top of those ranges and can go above them. Costs also rise around Black Friday, Christmas and the end of the financial year, when everyone is advertising at once.

## Ad spend versus management fee

These are two different costs, and it's worth being clear on both:

- **Ad spend** is what you pay Meta to show the ads. It should be charged **directly to your card**, in your own ad account.
- **The management fee** is what you pay someone to plan, write, design, run and report on the ads.

If an agency bundles the two into one number, ask how much of it actually reaches Meta. Some agencies add a margin on top of ad spend. That's not illegal, but you should know about it.

## How to tell if it's working

After about four weeks, look at three things:

1. **Cost per lead or booking.** Is it lower than what a new customer is worth to you?
2. **Lead quality.** Are these real enquiries, or people who never answer the phone?
3. **Tracking.** Can you actually see where each enquiry came from? If not, fix that before spending more. Without tracking you're guessing.

If the cost per lead works for your business, increase the budget slowly, by about 20 to 30 per cent at a time, and keep watching. If it doesn't, change one thing (the offer, the audience or the ad), not everything at once.
