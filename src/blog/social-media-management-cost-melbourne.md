If you've asked three people what social media management costs, you've probably heard three wildly different numbers. That's because "managing your socials" can mean anything from someone scheduling a few Canva posts to a team filming weekly video, replying to every comment and reporting on what it brought in.

So here's what the market actually looks like in 2026, and what you should expect at each price.

## What Melbourne small businesses actually pay

| Who does it | Typical monthly cost | What you usually get |
|---|---|---|
| Marketplace freelancer (Airtasker, Fiverr) | $300–$700 | Scheduled posts from your photos, light captioning |
| Experienced freelancer | $500–$1,500 | A content plan, 8–12 posts, basic graphics, some engagement |
| Small agency or practice | $700–$1,500 | Strategy, short-form video, captions, monthly reporting |
| Mid-size agency | $1,000–$2,500+ | Multiple platforms, more video, a dedicated account manager |

An audit of 140 Australian agencies published in June 2026 found the average published entry price for organic social media was about **$1,219 a month**, with a **median of $960**. One Melbourne agency publishes a starting point of roughly $600 a month for its basic small business package.

None of these numbers include **ad spend**. If someone quotes you "social media" and it includes running Facebook or Instagram ads, the money you pay Meta for the ads themselves sits on top of the management fee.

## What pushes the price up or down

Four things decide most of the price:

1. **How many platforms.** Instagram alone is a very different job from Instagram, Facebook, TikTok and LinkedIn.
2. **Video.** Short-form video (Reels, TikTok) takes far longer to plan, film and edit than a static post. It's usually the biggest jump in price, and usually worth it.
3. **Who creates the content.** If you supply photos and someone posts them, it's cheap. If they come on site to shoot, it isn't.
4. **Community management.** Replying to comments and DMs daily is real work. Some packages include it; many quietly don't.

## When it isn't worth paying for

This is the part most agencies won't tell you. Paying someone to post for you is a waste of money if:

- **Your website or booking process is broken.** More followers won't help if the person who clicks can't book.
- **You'd only be paying for "consistency".** Three posts a week with no point to them rarely moves anything. Fewer, better posts tied to an offer usually beat volume.
- **Nobody can tell you what it's for.** Before you sign, ask: "What should this get us in three months, and how will we know?" If the answer is "engagement" and nothing else, keep looking.

## Questions to ask before you sign

- Is there a lock-in contract? If so, why?
- Who owns the accounts and the content if we stop working together? (It should be you.)
- What exactly is included each month: posts, videos, replies, reports?
- Can I see a real monthly report you've sent another client?
- Is ad spend included, marked up, or paid by me directly?
